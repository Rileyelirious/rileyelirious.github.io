generating inquiry

Students will be able to generate and explore genuine lines of inquiry related to writing, language, literacy, and/or rhetoric.

Artifact 1 - From QQC 2: Grant Davies "Rhetorical Situation and its Constituents"

My quote from the reading is this: "Rhetors may invite audiences to accept new identities for themselves, offering readers a vision not of who they are but of who they could be. Readers who begin the discourse in one role may find themselves persuaded to adopt a new role, or they may refuse the roles suggested by the discourse."

I chose this quote because I believe it also has significance in our lives today with current events. Specifically, it makes me think about the role we play, or rather a large group has strives to not play, in the recent situation involving Flock cameras. The group of people that have taken it upon themselves to remove and obscure the vision of these cameras have effectively participated in what this quote speaks on; for instance, they've been told their role is to give up some of their privacy supposedly in exchange for more safety, but they refuse that role and take action to establish that.

A good question to build off that would be this: what situation in your life has created discourse that has influenced the role you play in said situation, and did you refuse or accept that role?

This was a good example of Generating Inquiry, because for this assignment, we had to do multiple things regarding the outcome.

Firstly, we had to read through the article keeping in mind the article previous to that, because the article read for this assignment was one that criticized and added on to a previous one.

With that knowledge from reading, I can then fulfill the outcome. I get a quote; then, I dove into that quote and why it was important. I explored inquiry I generated for myself, that being, why is this quote important?

After that exploration related to the article, I then generated new inquiry. For me, I also tried to explore this other inquiry, but it wasn't required for the assignment.

The outcome was fulfilled. The artifact depicts this, picking out a quote, generating inquiry and exploring it, and then generating inquiry off of that exploration once more.

Artifact 2 - QQC 1: Bitzer's "Rhetorical Situation"

The quote I will be picking out from "The Rhetorical Situation" by Lloyd Bitzer is this: "A tree does not obtain its character-as-tree from the soil, but rhetorical discourse, I shall argue, does obtain its character-as-rhetorical from the situation which generates it."

I chose it because I think using a metaphor is an interesting way that the author fleshed out the relationship between rhetorical discourse and the situation it spawns from. It helped me understand how a situation influences the rhetorical discourse itself, which in turn helped with the later concepts of the "constituents" of a rhetorical work and understanding how the situation dictates the audience and constraints within it. I think it is significant because of this heightened understanding it brings to the reader, especially with the use of the metaphor to connect it to something more grounded in reality rather than theory.

My question is, how did this quote help you personally understand the essay? Do you agree with my findings, not agree, or maybe have something to add onto them?

This was also a good example of generating inquiry. This is very similar to the previous artifact in that it is the same assignment type, QQC.

This artifact derived itself from the first article we read, Bitzer's "Rhetorical Situation". In it, I found a metaphor interesting and decided I'd use it as my quote. Not arbitrarily, no, it helped me understand concepts that the article set out to teach.

This fulfills part of the outcome because I first took my time to understand the article and then I generated inquiry, something like "How did this quote help me?"

I explored this inquiry by detailing how exactly it helped and how the quote was used.

I then asked another question to the audience, which may not be explorative, but still satisfies one of the parts of the outcome once again.

Overall, it generated inquiry, allowed me to explore it, and generate inquiry off of that exploration, succinctly fulfilling the outcome.
